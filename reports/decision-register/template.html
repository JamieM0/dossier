This test adds ratings, rather than direct feedback on a taste statement. It selects a wrong initial statement and supplies counterexamples. Of 28 eligible tests, 25 remove it, one replaces it, and two reduce recommendation accuracy too much. Three other viewers form no eligible wrong statement.

What these results do and do not establish · sources and definitions
  • Scope: improve Dossier’s existing personal model. Public reviews, tags, and the switching rule are comparison evidence. Their scores do not establish a repair to Dossier.
  • Diagnostic replays: holding one scoring contribution at its earlier value identifies a cause in a recorded session. It is not a tested algorithm for new viewers.
  • 108 real viewers: 31 in the original TMDB study, 47 in the confirmation, and 30 in the latest switching-rule test. Four evidence orders per viewer do not equal four people.
  • Different groups are not before-and-after model versions. Compare methods within a group. The latest 30 viewers reserve 40 hidden films each; the earlier groups reserve 60.
  • Hidden answers stay hidden during training. The CLI receives supplied ratings and public film details. The evaluator scores the resulting order against withheld personal ratings.
  • Public reviews mean average audience ratings. We did not analyse written review text. Tags mean genres and keywords matched to the person’s ratings.
  • Film-order accuracy: the fraction of liked-versus-disliked hidden pairs in the correct order. Ties receive half credit. Neutral ratings do not enter this measure.
  • Liked films per ten: clearly liked films among the first ten suggestions. A like means at least four stars; a dislike means 2.5 stars or fewer. Three and 3.5 stars are neutral.
  • No personal recommendation may be available at the start. The report counts empty suggestion sets as zero likes per ten and a 50% film-order score. The availability figure makes this visible.
  • Individual losses matter. Three film-order points is a review flag, not proof of a statistically certain loss. Film-order accuracy and the first-ten count can disagree.
  • Limited population: these are historical MovieLens ratings for films people chose to watch. Current public reviews postdate those ratings. This is not a live-user or whole-catalogue test.
  • Small gains need restraint. The candidate’s first 0.59-point gain came from selecting among 11 methods. On 47 new viewers, its gain was 0.95 points, with an estimated 0.33–1.60-point range. That group is now used, not untouched.
  • Independent experiments stay separate. No score here combines untested settings. Changing personal matching, questions, or damaged input does not simulate a new product configuration.
  • Sources: MovieLens ratings from GroupLens, under the MovieLens conditions. Film details from TMDB. This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.
  • Reproducibility: headlines use the saved group results. Individual curves use saved viewer averages. Any group made by selecting viewers uses those rounded averages. Source file checksums appear below.
